# Idea quality check

An idea should depend on the user's actual situation, not merely swap a noun
into a generic pitch. Evaluate the mechanism, constraints, audience, tradeoffs
and plausible first step.

- Replace vague categories with concrete behavior or material.
- Make options meaningfully different in approach, not just technology names.
- Preserve the requested number of options and level of practicality. Weirdness
  is useful when requested, not a universal quality requirement.
- Use specific names/numbers only when supplied, verified, or clearly fictional.
  Do not invent historical facts or endorsements to decorate a concept.
- Explain an honest failure mode and how the user could test the idea.
- Cut promotional language and claims of measured creativity without evidence.

Revise weak candidates as needed; there is no fixed quota of discarded drafts.
Do not narrate hidden drafts or force method-selection questions when the goal
already supplies enough direction. A simple useful idea can satisfy the request.
